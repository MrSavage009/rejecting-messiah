## The Quranic Standard: What God Says About Jesus

Before I critique the sects, I must establish the standard. What does the Quran actually say about Jesus? What is his status? What is his role? What are we commanded to believe about him?

The Quran calls Jesus al-Masih—the Messiah—in numerous verses. In 3:45, the angels announce to Mary "the Messiah, Jesus, son of Mary." In 4:171, he is called "the Messiah, Jesus, son of Mary, the Messenger of Allah." In 5:75, he is again named al-Masih. This title is not a verb. It cannot be conjugated into past, present, and future tenses. It is a permanent designation. The Quran never revokes it. It never says his messiahship expired. It never says he was the Messiah only for a time and then ceased to be.

The Quran states that Jesus was not killed or crucified, but that Allah raised him to Himself (4:157–158). The classical understanding is that he was raised alive, body and soul, to the heavens. He is not dead. He is not a memory. He is a living person, awaiting the appointed time.

The Quran ties him to the Hour. In 43:61, we read: "And indeed, he will be a sign for knowledge of the Hour, so be not in doubt of it, and follow Me. This is a straight path." The dominant interpretation is that this refers to the descent of Jesus before the Day of Resurrection. His return is a major sign of the Hour.

The Quran says that the People of the Book will believe in him before his death (4:159). It presents him as a sign for the people and a mercy from God (19:21). It says God made him and his mother a sign for the worlds (21:91).

And the Quran commands us to consider the Gospel. In 5:46, it says that Jesus was given the Gospel, "containing guidance and light, and confirming what was revealed in the Torah—a guide and a lesson to the God-fearing." In 5:47, it commands: "Let the people of the Gospel judge by what Allah has revealed therein." The Gospel is not dismissed. It is praised. It is called guidance and light.

So the Quranic standard is clear: Jesus is al-Masih, alive, present, returning. The Gospel is guidance and light. We are commanded to consider it. Any sect that fails to honor this standard is deviating from the Quran.

And yet the sects of Islam—Sunni, Shia, Salafi, Sufi, Quranist—each in its own way has failed to honor this standard. They have reduced Jesus to a past figure, a future abstraction, or a symbolic ideal. They have dismissed the Gospel as corrupted. They have ignored the Quran's own commands. And they have called this Islam. But it is not Islam. It is the Romanization of Islam—the replacement of the prophetic method with the method of empire. It is the abandonment of the Meccan method. It is the rejection of the sunnah of Jesus. And it is the reason why the Ummah is trapped in darkness. The Quranic standard is not a suggestion. It is a command. And the sects have disobeyed it.

How often do they reflect on the verses that present him as alive, present, and returning? ## The Shia Flaw: Imams Over the Messiah

Like Sunnis, Shia Muslims call Jesus a prophet and messenger. They honor him as al-Masih. But they also see him as subordinate to the Imams. Jesus is a prophet, but the Imams are more than prophets—they are the chosen ones, the purified ones, the ones whose suffering redeems the community.

This emphasis on the Imams has led to a marginalization of Jesus. Shia Muslims may revere Jesus, but they do not study the Gospel. They do not follow his sunnah. ## The Salafi Flaw: Literalism Without Love

Salafis reject many of the innovations and traditional practices of Sunni and Shia Islam. They emphasize the Quran and the authentic Hadith. They are often characterized by their strict literalism, their rejection of interpretation, and their willingness to declare others apostates.

Salafis claim to follow the Sunnah of Muhammad more faithfully than anyone else. They dress like the Prophet, pray like the Prophet, eat like the Prophet. But they ignore the Sunnah of Jesus. They do not study the Gospel. They dismiss the Bible as corrupted. They reduce Jesus to a prophet who came, delivered a message, and left.

Salafis are often the most vocal in their rejection of the Gospel. They say the Bible has been changed, that it is unreliable, that it cannot be trusted. But the Quran does not say this. The Quran says the Gospel is guidance and light. The Quran commands Christians to judge by it. If Salafis were truly faithful to the Quran, they would study the Gospel. They would consider its teachings. They would honor the Messiah.

Salafis also practice taqlid—not to the four schools, but to their own scholars. They follow the rulings of Ibn Taymiyyah, Ibn Abdul Wahhab, and contemporary Salafi scholars without question. They claim to reject blind following, but they engage in it constantly. They claim to follow the Quran, but they interpret it through the lens of their own ideology.

## Divorce: The Worst of Permissible Things

The Prophet said: "The most hated of permissible things to Allah is divorce." So divorce is permitted—but hated. It is the worst of halal things. It is a concession to human weakness, not a model for human life. And yet the four schools transformed this hated concession into a routine procedure. A man may divorce his wife unilaterally, without cause, without her consent, without a court. He says three words and it is done. She has no such power. She must seek a judge or return her dowry. The hated thing became easy. The exception became the norm.

The Church, meanwhile, made marriage indissoluble—but then invented annulments, and turned the sacrament into a bureaucratic labyrinth that trapped the abused and enriched the courts. Neither the sects nor the Church preserved the prophetic middle path: permanence as the ideal, divorce as a tragic last resort, and the dignity of both parties protected.

Muhammad called divorce "the most hated of permissible things." The sects made it easy. The Church made it impossible. Both betrayed the prophetic balance. The way of Jesus—and the way of Muhammad following Jesus—is the narrow path between the two: hold marriage sacred, make divorce difficult, and never let the law become a weapon against the vulnerable.

## The Hudud: Complexity and Mercy

The Quran prescribes punishments for certain crimes—theft, adultery, slander, murder. But the Quran also hedges these punishments with conditions so strict that they are nearly impossible to apply. For adultery, four witnesses are required—four people who saw the act itself. If four witnesses cannot be produced, the accuser is punished with eighty lashes for slander. The Prophet said: "Avoid the hudud as much as you can. If there is any doubt, let the punishment be avoided." He said: "Woe to the one who makes the hudud a means of oppression." The law was given—but the door to mercy was left wide open.

And yet the sects turned this narrow gate into a broad highway of punishment. They applied the hudud to the poor and the weak while the rich and powerful escaped. The law is real. But mercy is real too. The Quran holds both together. The sects dropped mercy.

The Church, meanwhile, had its own system of punishment—the Inquisition, the burning of heretics, the torture of dissidents—and it too forgot the mercy of Jesus. Jesus did not stone the woman caught in adultery. He said: "Let him who is without sin cast the first stone." One by one, her accusers walked away. And Jesus said: "Neither do I condemn you. Muhammad followed the same path. He upheld the law but opened the door to mercy. He said: "The most beloved of deeds to Allah is to forgive the one who wronged you." The sects have the hudud. Jesus has the woman caught in adultery. The sects have the executioner. Jesus has the one who says: "Neither do I condemn you." The sects have the law without mercy. ## The Prodigal Son: A Parable of Grace

The parable of the prodigal son is not just a story about forgiveness. It is a revelation of the Father's heart. A son takes his inheritance, squanders it on reckless living, and ends up feeding pigs. He returns home expecting to be made a servant. But his father runs to him—undignified for an elder—embraces him, robes him, puts a ring on his finger, and throws a feast. The elder brother protests: "I have served you all these years and never disobeyed. You never gave me a feast." The father says: "Son, you are always with me, and all that I have is yours. But we had to celebrate and be glad, because this brother of yours was dead and is alive again; he was lost and is found."

This is the scandal of grace. The father does not wait for the son to complete a legal procedure. He does not require restitution. He does not demand a period of probation. He runs. He embraces. He restores. And yet the sects of Islam turned this grace into a legal transaction. Repentance became a checklist. Forgiveness became conditional. The father became a judge, and the son became a defendant. The Church, meanwhile, turned grace into cheap grace—used it to abolish the law entirely, to say that obedience does not matter, that sin is irrelevant. Both betrayed the Father's heart.

Muhammad followed the sunnah of Jesus by balancing the two: the law is real, sin is real, consequences are real. But the door of mercy is always open. The father is always waiting. The son is always welcome. And the elder brother—who obeyed but resented—is gently rebuked: "Son, you are always with me."

The path of the believer is not the path of the elder brother, who obeyed without love. It is not the path of the younger son, who repented only when he was starving. It is the path of one who obeys out of love, repents out of humility, and trusts the Father's embrace. The sects have the checklist. The Church has the cheap grace. Jesus has the running father. Muhammad following Jesus has the balance: the law and the mercy, the justice and the embrace, the son who returns and the father who runs.

# Appendix B: The Teachings on Livelihood, Survival, and Integrity

The following is the full text of the teachings that form the missing link in this manifesto. They are presented in their general sense, without reference to contemporary figures or events. They are the psychological and spiritual framework for overcoming the slave mentality that keeps the Ummah bound to corrupt systems out of fear.

**1. "You Cannot Serve God and Mammon" (Matthew 6:24)**

The Principle: Mammon is not just money; it is the entire system of worldly power, wealth, and survival that demands your ultimate allegiance. It represents the worldly master that promises security in exchange for your soul. Jesus stated that a person cannot serve two masters. You cannot serve both Truth (God) and the System (Mammon).

The General Application: Every human being eventually faces a moment where the system demands they compromise their integrity to protect their livelihood, their status, or their safety. The system promises that if you just stay silent, just comply, just go along with the injustice, you will be rewarded. Jesus says this is a lie. You cannot split your loyalty. Eventually, the system will force you to choose. You will either sacrifice your truth to save your worldly position, or you will sacrifice your worldly position to save your truth.

**2. "Do Not Worry About Your Life" (Matthew 6:25-34)**

The Principle: "Therefore I tell you, do not worry about your life, what you will eat or drink; or about your body, what you will wear... Look at the birds of the air; they do not sow or reap or store away in barns, and yet your heavenly Father feeds them. Are you not much more valuable than they?"

The General Application: The system controls people primarily through fear—the fear of losing your job, your home, your reputation, or your community. The slave mentality operates on the belief that survival is the highest good. Jesus dismantles this by pointing out that life is more than food, and the body more than clothes. He is saying: stop making decisions based on the fear of losing your livelihood. Stop asking, "What will happen to me if I speak up?" Start asking, "What is right?" Trust that if you put Truth first, your basic needs will be met. The anxiety about survival is the chain the system uses to keep you bound. Jesus says: break the chain. Trust God with the outcome.

**3. "What Good Is It to Gain the Whole World, Yet Forfeit Your Soul?" (Mark 8:36)**

The Principle: You can have all the success, all the applause, all the money, and all the comfort the world has to offer—but if you had to sell your soul (your integrity, your witness, your truth) to get it, you have lost everything.

The General Application: The world constantly offers a trade: "Give me your silence, and I will give you safety. Give me your compliance, and I will give you success. Give me your soul, and I will give you the world." Many people take the deal. They gain the world—the career, the social standing, the financial security—but they lose themselves in the process. Their witness is gone. Their voice is gone. They become hollow. Jesus says: the soul is the only thing that matters. If you lose it, nothing else you gain is worth it.

**4. "Do Not Fear Those Who Kill the Body" (Matthew 10:28)**

The Principle: "Do not be afraid of those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul. Rather, be afraid of the One who can destroy both soul and body in hell."

The General Application: The master in any oppressive system—whether it is a narcissistic parent, a corrupt employer, a hostile institution, or an authoritarian government—can only take away your worldly comforts. The ultimate tragedy is not dying; the ultimate tragedy is living without your soul.

**5. "Where Your Treasure Is, There Your Heart Will Be Also" (Matthew 6:19-21)**

The Principle: "Do not store up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moths and vermin destroy, and where thieves break in and steal. But store up for yourselves treasures in heaven... For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also."

The General Application: Whatever you value most is what you will be a slave to. The Principle: Jesus told a story about a rich man who built bigger barns to store his wealth. He said to himself, "You have plenty of grain laid up for many years. Take life easy; eat, drink and be merry." But God said to him, "You fool! This very night your life will be demanded from you. Then who will get what you have prepared for yourself?"

The General Application: The system trains people to build bigger barns. Get the promotion. Buy the bigger house. Secure the retirement. Build the platform. Hoard the wealth.
